# Rolling Tides Universe — Campsite & Inn Bible

Recovered from the 19 Sep 2026 discussion, for the 3D life-sim/RPG direction. Recorded verbatim in substance.

## 1. Core philosophy
Travel is not `town → loading screen → town`. The space between settlements is part of the life simulation:
wilderness, roads, ruins, monsters, travellers, and the places people stop to rest. Campsites are simultaneously
rest points, building locations, social spaces, businesses and adventure infrastructure.

    Campsite → Reinforced Camp → Traveler Shelter → Roadside Inn → Upgraded Inn

Upgrading is optional. A five-star campsite may stay a campsite forever.

## 2. Campsites are NOT normal properties
Property limits (roughly 4 homes + 2 major land plots) do **not** consume campsite slots. A campsite is part of
the world's travel network, not another private house. The player secures and develops it, but other travellers
still use it. **You own the improvements. You don't own the road.**

## 3. Basic campsite
Claimed with little more than a campfire, sleeping area, seating and personal storage. Improved by hand with
tents, bedrolls, cooking station, chairs, tables, lighting, water, weather protection, fences, storage,
decoration, animal/monster protection, training equipment. These are not stat bonuses — **you must actually see
travellers using them**: arrive, drop pack, sit by the fire, cook, talk, sleep, move on at dawn.

## 4. Comfort rating (★–★★★★★)
Not "10 furniture pieces = 5 stars" — assessed on real usability: beds, shelter, warmth, food, seating,
lighting, safety, cleanliness, and whether NPCs can actually reach and use everything. Reassessed roughly every
three in-game days, so the rating behaves like a reputation rather than a live counter.

## 5. Ratings affect who stops
Poor camps draw desperate or broke travellers. Good camps draw merchants, adventurers, families, craftspeople,
quest characters, mages, Gear specialists, rare travellers. This feeds the Power Bible without another skill
menu: a wandering Dark Magic teacher, a Gear specialist, or a Beastfolk traveller who knows something about your
Race Transformation may simply be there in the morning.

## 6. The Campfire Inn system
The inn is built physically around the original campfire, which stays as the historical centre — not
"upgrade to Inn Level 1". The fireplace in the successful inn is the same fire you slept beside the first time
you crossed the region.

## 7. Inn progression
1. **Campsite** — basic survival, player maintains everything
2. **Reinforced Camp** — permanent structures, better shelter, storage, cooking, defences
3. **Traveler Shelter** — intentionally supports NPC travellers; multiple beds, cooking, basic services
4. **Roadside Inn** — permanent building, guest rooms, kitchen, common area, storage, staff, revenue
5. **Upgraded Inn** — specialisation: better rooms and food, staff, entertainment, merchant services,
   stables/creature facilities, training areas, possible magical/Gear services

## 8. Inns must not print money
Building requires materials, carpentry skill/tier, money, reputation/permission, construction time and possibly
local requirements. Operating costs money. Profit depends on location (a crossroads beats an isolated cliff),
traveller traffic, comfort, food, safety, reputation, bed count and operating costs. **Revenue ≠ profit.**

## 9. Player labour → automation
A new inn is not passive income. At first *you* are the innkeeper: cook, clean, repair, restock, check guests
in, maintain rooms, protect travellers. You can be the adventurer who just unlocked Lightning Body and still be
late downstairs because somebody wants breakfast. Later you hire an operator, then staff, and automation becomes
something the player earned.

## 10. Inns become NPC anchors
Once an inn exists, NPC schedules can treat it as a real destination: `Home → Road → Campsite/Inn → Road →
Destination`. Travelling NPCs stop overnight, party members suggest stopping, merchants pass through, someone you
met before is unexpectedly downstairs, rumours travel. An inn is an **information node for the simulation**, not
another shop.

## 11. Adventure integration
Rest restores Power Bible systems: stamina, vital energy, power recovery, physical condition. Food gives extra
recovery/buffs. Training at camp contributes to mastery; mages study by the fire; Gear users maintain equipment;
party members socialise; races may have unique camping behaviour; dangerous areas make camping itself risky.

    Travel → Explore → Fight → Camp → Eat → Recover → Socialise → Train → Sleep → Continue

instead of `fight → potion → fight → potion → town`.

## 12. Home vs Inn vs Camp
**Adventure changes the meaning of home, but never replaces it.** Home is your actual life; an inn is temporary
civilisation while travelling; a camp is a temporary home in the wilderness. However large the inn empire grows,
coming back to the hometown must still feel different.

## Connection to the Power Bible
BODY users recover through food, sleep and rest. MAGIC users study, meditate and restore Soul Energy at camp.
GEAR users repair, modify and prepare. RACE mastery can be trained through culturally appropriate camp
activities and mentors. High-rated camps attract unusual travellers, so an entire storyline can arrive while you
are simply running your inn — the RPG emerging from living in the world rather than dragging the player out of it.

## Multiplayer tie-in (2026-09-23)
Guest houses in towns and villages that visiting players can claim as their own, and campsites/inns as the same
kind of claimable, developable travel infrastructure. This maps directly onto the co-op permission roles
(VISITOR / MEMBER / OWNER) — see `MULTIPLAYER_PLAN.md`.
